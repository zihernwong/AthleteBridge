Educate yo'self fr 📚

The economic terms that lowkey control everything — your portfolio, your rent, your whole bag. Explained no cap.

📊
Term 01
GDP
The economy's report card, no cap
Gross Domestic Product is the total value of everything a country produces in a year — goods, services, all of it. Think of it as the economy's GPA. When GDP grows, businesses are printing, people have jobs, and the market usually follows. When GDP goes negative two quarters in a row, that's a recession era and portfolios get cooked. The 2020 COVID crash dropped GDP by nearly 3% — the worst since the Great Depression.
📈 GDP rising → bull market energy 📉 GDP negative → recession szn
🔥
Term 02
Inflation
Why your $20 hits different than it used to
Inflation is prices going up over time across the whole economy. A little bit (~2%) is actually healthy and what the Fed targets. But when inflation goes 8%+ like in 2022? Everything gets expensive — groceries, rent, gas — and the Fed has to step in with rate hikes that tank growth stocks. Moderate inflation is fine for equities. Runaway inflation is the villain arc.
📈 ~2% inflation → market slays 😐 3–5% → market gets nervous 📉 8%+ → bear market szn
🧾
Term 03
CPI
The receipts on how expensive life is getting
Consumer Price Index tracks the price of a fixed basket of everyday items — groceries, rent, gas, clothes. It's literally how the government measures inflation. Every month when CPI data drops, the whole market reacts in real time. CPI coming in hotter than expected = red day. CPI cooling down = green day. Wall Street watches this number like it's a Kendrick album drop.
📈 CPI cooling → rally incoming 📉 CPI hot → market dips on the news
🏦
Term 04
Interest Rates
The Fed's main character energy that moves everything
The Federal Reserve sets the benchmark interest rate to control inflation. When rates go up, borrowing gets expensive — mortgages, car loans, business credit all cost more. Tech and growth stocks especially hate high rates because their future profits get discounted hard. The Fed went from 0.25% → 5.5% in under 2 years (2022–2023) — one of the fastest hike cycles ever, and the market felt it fr.
📈 Rate cuts → market goes brr 📉 Rate hikes → growth stocks cooked

How it all connects 🔗

These four terms aren't separate — they're one big chain reaction. Here's how it plays out.

🟢 Bull Scenario
1
GDP grows strong — economy is healthy, companies printing money
2
Inflation stays near 2% — prices rising but not crazy
3
Fed holds or cuts rates — borrowing stays cheap, business invests
4
CPI stays chill — consumers keep spending, confidence high
✓
Result: Stocks go up. Portfolio secured. 💰
🔴 Bear Scenario
1
Inflation spikes — supply chain issues, too much money printed
2
CPI data comes in hot — everyday prices going up fast
3
Fed hikes interest rates aggressively to cool inflation down
4
GDP starts slowing — businesses borrow less, hiring freezes
✗
Result: Stocks drop. Market in its villain era. 📉
